    Why you can trust the recommendations (sources)
    

      
One fixed wake time, seven days a week. The circadian clock sets itself by regularity; the gap between body time and social time ("social jetlag") tracks with worse sleep and daytime function. A single anchor beats any clever bedtime. Wittmann et al. 2006, Chronobiology International; core of behavioral insomnia treatment.

      
Sleep need from age-band consensus, refined by your own history. Expert consensus: adults 18–64 need 7–9 h (65+: 7–8 h; teens: 8–10 h); adults regularly under 7 h show measurable impairment. Your unconstrained ("vacation") duration locates you inside that band. Hirshkowitz et al. 2015, Sleep Health (NSF); Watson et al. 2015, Sleep (AASM/SRS).

      
Chronotype computed, not guessed. Mid-sleep on free days, corrected for the sleep debt you carry into them (MSFsc), is the standard field measure of circadian phase — it's how "lark vs. owl" is measured in the research literature, and it's derived purely from the times you gave. Roenneberg et al. 2003, Journal of Biological Rhythms (Munich ChronoType Questionnaire).

      
Morning light, evening dim. Light is the clock's master input: bright light shortly after waking pulls your rhythm earlier; light in the hours before bed pushes it later. That's why the plan puts you outside within ~30 min of waking and dims screens before lights-out. Khalsa et al. 2003, Journal of Physiology (human phase-response curve).

      
Caffeine cutoff with margin. 400 mg taken a full 6 h before bed still cut measured sleep by over an hour in a controlled trial. The plan sets your cutoff ~9 h before lights-out. Drake et al. 2013, Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine.

      
Gradual shifts only. The clock advances reliably by small daily steps, not leaps — the plan moves you 15 min every 2 nights, which is comfortably inside what morning light + fixed wake can hold onto.

      
Out of bed if not sleepy. Stimulus control — keeping bed paired with sleep, not with lying awake — is among the best-validated single components of insomnia treatment. Bootzin 1972; CBT-I is first-line care for chronic insomnia per Qaseem et al. 2016, Annals of Internal Medicine (ACP guideline).

      
Naps are borrowed sleep pressure. Sleep drive builds the longer you're awake (the homeostatic process); a late or long nap spends it early. Short (<20 min) and before ~14:30 if you must, none during the measure phase. Borbély 1982 two-process model.

      
Where this tool stops. Loud snoring, gasping, unrefreshing sleep despite adequate hours, or severe daytime sleepiness are clinical signs (e.g. sleep apnea) that scheduling cannot fix — that's a conversation with a clinician, and treating it changes everything else. This app is a self-experiment aid, not medical advice.
